# ADR-008 — Use a baseline and one ready-made candidate model behind an evaluation gate

**Status:** Accepted (2026-11-25)

## The problem

The course excludes training or fine-tuning of our own models, yet a forecast that is not evaluated cannot be trusted, and our data is a small synthetic fixture. We need to know whether any model is better than doing almost nothing.

## Our decision

Keep two forecasting methods behind the adapter: a baseline that repeats the last observed daily value, and one ready-made candidate chosen during implementation among options that need no training by us (a pretrained zero-shot forecaster or a classical method with run-time parameter estimation). A rolling-origin backtest stores MAE and MAPE for both. The candidate becomes active only if its MAE is not worse than the baseline's; a worse live error for three consecutive evaluations returns the registry to the baseline and alerts the operator. The operator can pin or roll back a model. A sanity guardrail checks every forecast before it is stored.

## Options we considered

- **Train our own neural network:** outside the scope, and the data does not support it.
- **Use a single model without a baseline:** no way to tell whether it helps.
- **Choose a named product now:** the choice should follow the evaluation, not precede it.
- **Evaluate only once, before release:** prices drift; live comparison with the baseline is cheap.

## What this means for the project

We need a model registry, an evaluation job and a drift check. With 30 days of synthetic data the backtest has few origins and its differences may be within noise; the report must say so. Evaluation jobs are optional batches that wait for GREEN mode, with a 7-day maximum deferral.
