# ADR-002 — Use PostgreSQL and retain where prices came from

**Status:** Accepted (2026-11-25)  
**Revision note:** Extended in revision 2 with operational state (jobs, carbon, policy, mode, traces).

## The problem

An offer is meaningless without its retailer, timestamp, currency and exact laptop variant, and importing the same file twice must not create a second copy. The adaptive design also needs durable operational state: forecast jobs with a deadline that retries cannot reset, carbon samples, the policy and the current operating mode, and assistant traces.

## Our decision

Store raw import batches as received (with a checksum), products, retailers, dated price observations, forecast runs, models, forecast jobs, execution logs, energy samples (carbon intensity, price, renewable flag), policy versions, the operating mode, placement decisions, drift checks and assistant traces in PostgreSQL. Raw, validated and derived data are kept as separate layers ([Data engineering](../data-engineering.md)). Enforce foreign keys and a compound uniqueness constraint on observation identity including source provenance. Use idempotent upserts for imports and lease-based claims for jobs. Keep ownership, deadlines and the mode in rows, never in process memory.

## Options we considered

- **CSV or JSON files only:** easy to inspect but unsuitable for concurrent queries, leases and durable job and policy state.
- **Document database:** flexible, but the relationships and constraints are known in advance and benefit from relational integrity.
- **Separate stores for prices, jobs and configuration:** isolates concerns but multiplies operations; one database with clear tables is enough at this scale.
- **Configuration only in ConfigMaps:** simple, but a change would need a deployment and gives no audit trail, which conflicts with runtime reconfiguration (ADR-004).

## What this means for the project

Schema design, migrations and backups become our responsibility. The benefit is an audit trail for each price, reliable repeated imports, a single source of truth for the policy and the mode, and consistent job ownership. PostgreSQL on a persistent volume is acceptable for the demonstration; a managed database would be preferable in production. A full database outage is not covered without a separate read cache.
