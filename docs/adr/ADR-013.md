# ADR-013 — Adapt in two layers: behaviour and workload placement

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-25), to be confirmed by the authors

## The problem

The course demonstrated two levels of energy awareness: the code switches its behaviour by price, and the application **redeploys itself** to where energy is cheaper or cleaner. The demonstration used labelled nodes, taints and tolerations, a scheduler that polled a price endpoint and a Helm chart. Our operating modes cover only the first layer.

## Our decision

Add a second layer. The cluster has two node pools, labelled *standard* (fast, default) and *eco* (simulated low-carbon or low-price node group). The Policy Engine stores a placement target with the operating mode (eco for AMBER, RED or when renewable energy is available, standard otherwise). A Placement Controller (a Deployment installed with a Helm chart) applies it by tainting the non-target pool with `energy-role=blocked:NoExecute` and removing the taint from the target pool. Flexible workloads (forecast and evaluation Jobs, the local model runtime) carry no toleration and are therefore evicted and rescheduled onto the target pool; the request path, the database, the broker and monitoring tolerate the taint and never move. Safety rules: only change taints when the decision changes, keep a dwell time of 15 minutes, require a ready node in both pools and never leave zero schedulable nodes. The speed difference between the pools is measured, not assumed.

## Options we considered

- **Behaviour only (revision 2):** simple but shows no relocation.
- **Node selectors or affinity set at deployment time:** needs a redeploy for every change; taints can move running pods.
- **Multiple regions or clusters:** the real-world analogue, but too much for the course and it raises data-location questions.
- **Cluster autoscaler or scheduler plugins:** powerful, but opaque for the demonstration.

## What this means for the project

Evicting a running Job wastes work; jobs are idempotent and leased, so the effect is a retry before the deadline. The controller needs cluster permissions (least privilege, a dedicated service account). The pools are simulated: the carbon and price benefit is an outcome to measure, not a claim.
