# ADR-017 — Store the price watch on the server under a pseudonymous device identifier

**Status:** Proposed (2026-11-25), to be confirmed by the authors. Implemented as an isolated module (`src/shopping/watch.py`) so that it can be removed or replaced without touching other components.

## The problem

FR-06 asked for a price watch without an account. Revision 2 kept it entirely in the browser: the client compared the target with the current price on the next visit. The supplied sequence diagram "Monitor prices and trigger alerts" and the supplied use-case diagram (Monitoring) describe a server-side watchlist with a scheduled check and stored alerts. The two designs conflict. A purely client-side watch cannot see a dip that happens and ends between two visits, cannot deduplicate, and cannot record what was checked; on the other hand it stores nothing on the server.

## Our decision

The watch (laptop, target price, enabled flag) is stored on the server under a random device identifier that the browser generates; the server keeps no account, name or contact detail. The **Watch Monitor** component offers `IWatchlist` to the Shopping API and `IWatchCheck` to the time trigger. A CronJob checks the watches every 30 minutes (a proposal): it takes the lowest latest offer per laptop, ignores observations older than 48 hours, and stores one alert per episode together with its deduplication key in a single transaction. An episode ends when a fresh price rises above the target, and a new target also starts a new episode. Alerts are shown in the application; there is no email or push. The shortlist stays in the browser.

## Options we considered

- **Browser only (revision 2):** nothing on the server, but dips between visits are missed and nothing is recorded.
- **Server-side with accounts and email or push:** the most useful, but it needs accounts, consent and a delivery channel, which the project excludes.
- **Server-side, device-scoped, in-app alerts (chosen):** catches dips and records them, at the cost of a small amount of pseudonymous data and one more component.

## What this means for the project

One more component, two tables (watches, alerts) and one CronJob; a limit of 20 watches per device and a retention period for inactive devices (proposed: 90 days, not yet implemented) keep the data small. The device identifier is a pseudonym, not anonymous data, so it must be deletable and must not be combined with other data. An alert reaches the shopper only when the application is opened. The check is a pull job and uses the same persisted-state-plus-idempotence approach as the import and the forecast jobs.
